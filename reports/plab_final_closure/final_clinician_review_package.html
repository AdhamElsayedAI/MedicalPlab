PLAB-CARD-0004

A 68-year-old woman with a history of type 2 diabetes mellitus and hypertension is diagnosed with newly discovered non-valvular atrial fibrillation on an ambulatory 24-hour ECG. She is asymptomatic with a heart rate of 78 beats/min. Her blood pressure is 134/82 mmHg, and echocardiography shows mild left atrial enlargement with a normal left ventricular ejection fraction (58%). She has no prior history of stroke, thromboembolism, or bleeding. According to UK clinical practice guidelines (NICE NG196), which of the following is the most appropriate stroke prevention strategy?

  1. A. Aspirin 75 mg once daily
  2. B. Clopidogrel 75 mg once daily
  3. C. Dual antiplatelet therapy with aspirin and clopidogrel
  4. D. Direct-acting oral anticoagulant (DOAC, e.g. apixaban)
  5. E. No antithrombotic therapy required

Explanation: Her CHA2DS2-VASc score is 4 (age 65-74, hypertension, diabetes and sex category). NICE NG196 recommends a direct-acting oral anticoagulant for atrial fibrillation with a score of 2 or above, taking bleeding risk into account.

PLAB-CARD-0005

A 72-year-old man presents to the medical assessment unit with palpitations and mild fatigue over the past three weeks. Examination reveals an irregularly irregular pulse at 128 beats/min and blood pressure 138/84 mmHg. An ECG confirms atrial fibrillation with rapid ventricular response. He is clinically euvolemic with clear lung fields and no signs of heart failure. Which of the following is the most appropriate first-line pharmacological agent for rate control in this hemodynamically stable patient?

  1. A. Digoxin monotherapy
  2. B. Amiodarone
  3. C. Flecainide
  4. D. Atropine
  5. E. A standard beta-blocker

Explanation: NICE NG196 recommends either a standard beta-blocker or a rate-limiting calcium-channel blocker as initial rate-control monotherapy. A standard beta-blocker is the only listed option matching that recommendation.
